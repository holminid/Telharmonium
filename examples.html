Recomposing the past

It's better to recreate past musical forms symbolically than statistically.

Why these examples of near-forgotten, even obsolete, musical styles? Why not something from this century, something you would hear coming out of a loud car stereo or in a night club?

In short, Telharmonium considers the present as being built on the past and the building blocks of today's music were already in place back in 1990 - the "future" of those heady days is already in the past. But by recombining and transforming the musical traits of yesteryear, we can build any musical form - going past the stuck and self-referential present to shape the music of tomorrow.

The Telharmonium Composer does not just generate notation or scores, but knows how they should be played, going way beyond expression and modulation in the process. The affective-behavioural-cognitive approach of Telharmonium Labs enables and challenges composers to deliver material that serves the evolutionary functions of music and musicality: supporting individual and social wellbeing, group cohesion, mood modification and alleviation of anxiety. This is possible with the use of a multidimensional musical world model and model of musical appreciation and preference - not one that looks at your streaming history but one that finds out what you actually like.

Orchestral / Symphonic

1914–1918 Symphony

Experiments in Lyrical Cyclic Form, with 36 MIDI GM2 tracks, with some divisi or sections. Individual voices are from physically modeled SWAM plugins. The aim of generating these was to define forms for further use in new compositions.

1914 Lyrical Symphony

1914: The first iteration of the recapitulation movement seemed somewhat tense with its use of the Lydian Chromatic Concept for harmonic language, foreshadowing was used create suspense.

1918 Lyrical Symphony

1918: The second iteration of the same recapitulation movement omits Lydian Chromaticism for a more simple harmonic progression, this time using relative scales that follow Charpentier's affective tonality. The result, with it's more emphasised brass, is almost (too) jazzy, but perhaps fitting a post-WW1 era.

Blues / Delta

1920 Delta Blues

Delta blues, derived from the Black Atlantic lineage with sub-Saharan traits, remains a source of influence touching everything from jazz to funk, soul, disco and house, even Detroit techno. This study is shaped by slide-guitar gestures, rapid figurations around sustained notes, and a narrative movement of a humid Southern afternoon going towards twilight.

Jazz / Quartet

1965 Post-bop Quartet

A small-group post-bop study in interacting instrumental phrases, harmonic movement and rhythmic interplay, this form bridges bebop or hard bop with later forms like harmolodic funk and free jazz. The second part introduces a fourth performer on piano, retaining the composition while taking the new addition into account. Note the compound logic triggering overblow on the physically modeled tenor sax.

1980–1985 Italo Disco

Italo disco emerged as a combination of Krautrock, Space music, post-Disco and Synthpop, even Electro and Post-punk. The four versions explore how changes in instrumentation and musical style can shift the mood of Italo disco, from the early hybrid band setup to more pop-oriented crossovers and variants that approach the club music of the late 1980s.

1980 Early hybrid electronic band

1980: Early Italo disco predated digital synthesizers and sequencing,often featuring session musicans and manually layered string machines.

Italian soundtrack miniature

Luna Park After Dark

A winter evening on the Adriatic. The rides have stopped, but nobody’s told the band. A lone accordion gives way to muted trumpet and reeds, with walking bass and woodblock knocks lending the middle section a slightly wobbly swing. It’s an exercise in wistfulness: when the opening tune returns, it sounds like a memory of the evening we’ve just heard.

ABOUT THESE RECORDINGS

These admittedly uneven recordings document ongoing development and will be updated with more examples. Instead of presenting production skills or audio quality, they demonstrate the structural and generative features of Telharmonium Composer's semantic and symbolic capabilities. Preparation varies by track. Some recordings are based directly on multitrack MIDI exports, while others include arrangement edits made before recording in Cubasis. Luna Park After Dark is a hand-arranged study rather than a direct Composer export.
